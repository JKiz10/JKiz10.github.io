What an Interior Designer Costs in Houston

Houston designers charge in one of four ways: by the hour, a flat design fee for a defined scope, a percentage of the project budget, or cost plus a markup on what they buy for you.

We charge by the hour. The onsite design consultation is $300 an hour. Contracted design time, once a project is under way, is $175 an hour. Furnishings, materials and construction are quoted separately, and they are the larger part of any renovation.

Most firms in this market publish no figure at all. Ours have been on the process page since it was written, and this page explains what sits around them.

Before you compare two proposals

The four ways designers charge

They are not interchangeable, and two firms quoting the same project can produce very different numbers without either of them being wrong.

Hourly
A stated rate for time
You pay for the hours the work takes. Easy to verify against an invoice, harder to forecast at the start. It suits a scope that is still moving.
Flat design fee
One price for a defined scope
Agreed up front, which makes it predictable. It is only as firm as the definition of the scope, so ask what happens when the scope grows.
Percentage of project
A share of what you spend
The fee is a proportion of construction and furnishings. It scales with the project, which also means the fee rises as the budget does.
Cost plus
Trade price plus a markup
The designer buys at trade pricing and adds a stated percentage. What you pay for a sofa is the trade price plus that markup, not the retail price.

Three questions compare firms better than an hourly rate does. Which of the four do you use. What is inside the fee and what is billed separately. What happens to the fee if the scope grows.

The bigger number

What the renovation costs, as opposed to the design

When people ask what a designer costs, they usually mean what the project costs. Design time is a fraction of it.

Houston kitchen remodels cluster in the mid $30,000s on average, and two independent methods agree within about eight per cent. Houston building permit data puts the average kitchen project at $36,841 against a national average of $46,623 (Block Renovation, January 2026). A sample of 1,054 recent projects across the Houston, The Woodlands and Sugar Land metro puts it at $34,100 (RenovCost, June 2026). Houston runs modestly below the national figure on both.

Mid range work in those datasets lands around $28,300 to $41,300, which buys semi-custom cabinets, quartz counters and stainless appliances. Drawn millwork and slab stone, which is what the kitchens in this portfolio are, sit in the tier above: around $62,900 and up, with no ceiling above it.

We do not publish a figure for a primary bath. The Houston sources we could find disagree by a factor of three and one of them contradicts itself inside a single page, so there is no number here worth repeating. We will give you a real one at the consultation, for your room.

What moves the number

Three things that change a Houston budget

Scope that crosses into architecture or engineering

Texas statute draws this line rather than leaving it to custom. Under the Texas Occupations Code, Chapter 1053, Section 1053.003, interior design registration does not authorise planning or designing architectural interior construction, engineering a building system, or practising architecture or engineering. Moving a load bearing wall or re-routing mechanical, electrical or plumbing work means an architect or an engineer alongside the designer, and a separate fee. Knowing that at the consultation is cheaper than discovering it at framing.

Review, where the address is subject to it

The Heights has three historic districts, not one: East, West and South, designated between 2007 and 2010. A Certificate of Appropriateness applies to work that alters the exterior appearance of a designated property, and the Houston Archaeological and Historical Commission meets once a month, with a complete application due twenty two days before the meeting. In River Oaks the Architectural Review Committee sees plans, elevations, site layouts and landscaping before anything is built. Neither changes an interior fee by itself; both change the calendar, and a calendar is a budget.

Lead times, which are longer than people expect

Custom millwork, stone and upholstered pieces drive more of a schedule than demolition does. It is why we specify early and why procurement sits inside the firm rather than with the contractor. A design that is approved but waiting on a single backordered vanity is the most expensive kind of delay there is.

What people ask about cost

Do you charge a percentage of the project?

No. We charge hourly, at the two published rates, so what you pay tracks the hours the project actually takes rather than the size of the budget. A percentage fee rises when the budget rises, which is worth understanding before you compare two proposals.

What is not included in the design fee?

Everything that is not design time. Furniture, lighting, plumbing fixtures, tile, stone, cabinetry, appliances, fabric, freight, receiving, storage and the construction itself are all quoted separately. Design time is a fraction of what a renovation costs.

Sometimes, and Texas statute draws the line. We work with both regularly and will say so at the consultation.
